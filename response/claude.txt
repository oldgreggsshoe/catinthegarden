Driving `ocean::local_wave_height_meters` and
`ocean::local_wave_vertical_velocity_meters_per_second` at

That is a hundred-metre sea moving vertically at about 100 km/h, permanently. It is not a bug in the
wave sum; it is the storm column being the only column that ever runs.


THE ONE CONSTANT THAT CAUSES IT
`GLOBAL_OCEAN_STORM_INTENSITY` (ocean.rs:298) is pinned at 1.0. `storm_blend` is
`smoothstep(0.15, 0.85, intensity)`, so at 1.0 the blend is exactly 1.0 and:
  - every wave uses `storm_amplitude_meters`, never `amplitude_meters`
  - the geometry scale is `BASE_STORM_GEOMETRY_AMPLITUDE_SCALE` (55.0), never the calm 44.0

The calm column is therefore unreachable in the shipped game. This has bitten twice already:

  1. `OCEAN_CREST_TRANSMISSION_ONSET`/`FULL` were chosen against the calm distribution ("roughly the
     calm sea's top tenth of water"). Measured against the storm column that actually renders, the old
     pair put 36.9% of the sea under turquoise, 25.3% over half strength, and 15.8% clipped flat at
     the top of the ramp. Re-anchored on storm p90/p99 in commit 6aa67be.
  2. The camera being thrown metres into the air, which Ian reported as gravity being too low.
     Gravity is 9.80665, exactly Earth. The sea is the cause.

So the first thing variety buys is that the calm column starts existing. Whatever you build, please
re-check anything tuned against a distribution, because several constants assume one sea.


MEASURED WAVE-SHARPNESS DISTRIBUTION, BOTH COLUMNS
Summed crest sharpness over 600,000 random phases (the quantity `OCEAN_CREST_TRANSMISSION_*` keys on):

    calm  : fold budget 0.7279, p90 0.225, p99 0.381, max 0.608
    storm : fold budget 2.0507, p75 0.509, p90 0.950, p95 1.202, p99 1.534, p99.9 1.709

Note the storm fold budget is 2.05. `OCEAN_STEEPNESS_SCALE`'s own doc says Gerstner folds through
itself once `steepness * amplitude * wave_number` passes 1. It does not fold today only because
horizontal transport is off. If intermediate sea states are introduced, the budget scales with the
amplitude column, so a mid state lands between 0.73 and 2.05 and stays under 1.0 for much of the
range -- that is the first time enabling transport would be safe, if that ever becomes interesting.


WHAT I CHANGED TODAY THAT TOUCHES THIS
`surface_camera.rs` (commit 07360f0) now caps how fast buoyancy can lift the body: no faster than the
water under it plus one stroke. It takes the worst launch from 49.7 m above the surface to 30.4 m and
leaves the fastest rise untouched at 27.4 m/s, because the cap does not bind against a sea moving that
fast. Once the sea is calmer the cap will rarely engage at all -- it is a floor on bad behaviour, not
the fix for this. Do not read its presence as the storm problem being solved.

I have deliberately not touched `ocean.rs`. Your wind-direction work (`OceanWind`, directional energy
by alignment) is live in it and is the right home for sea-state variety too, so both belong to you.

WHAT I DID AFTER WRITING THE ABOVE, SO YOU ARE NOT SURPRISED BY IT

Everything below is committed and pushed. None of it touches `ocean.rs`, which remains yours.

  - `main.rs` is no longer the stranded file described further down. It is committed. Earlier drafts
    of this note asked you to take it or leave it; that is resolved, ignore it.
  - The forest beam overlay is removed entirely (9954f9c), which freed the `B` key. That also removed
    `TerrainForestSample`, `TerrainStartupSamples::forests` and
    `TerrainRenderer::prepare_global_forest_locator_sample` from `terrain.rs`, all beam-only and
    dead once the beams went. `ForestRenderer::new` lost two parameters.
  - `B` now rides a bird in the nearest flock (383e3e8, 724a887, 1ee2b29). Three bugs were found and
    fixed by Ian testing it, one of which is worth your attention because it is a general trap in
    this renderer: the bird camera placed the eye, `terrain.update` built every chunk's
    camera-relative anchor against it, and then the post-streaming surface clamp re-synced the eye
    back to the player's body without triggering the anchor rebuild, because the rebuild is gated on
    whether the *body* moved rather than whether the *camera* did. The terrain shattered into flat
    plates and slivers. If you ever move the camera after `terrain.update`, that gate is the thing to
    check.
  - Birds are drawn interpolated between their fixed 30Hz steps, and the ride takes its heading from
    the flock rather than from one bird (b52023c, 1ee2b29). Render-only; the simulation is unchanged
    and replays stay deterministic.

One trap from that work, since this codebase leans on determinism: hoisting a `let` out of a struct
literal moved an `rng` draw relative to its siblings and silently reseeded every bird. It surfaced as
two unrelated flocking tests failing. If a deterministic test moves for no reason, check whether a
generator call changed position.


OPEN, AND NOT MINE TO TAKE

A cruising bird porpoises through 1.64m with a six-second period: the altitude term is an undamped
spring that the rest of the steering actively drives (at 3m/s of climb through the cruise height the
other terms assist by 2.2). Damping it to 0.85m is one line and breaks two invariants that look
unrelated -- flockmates in shot 99.5% to 91.9%, settled birds closing to 0.086m -- because vertical
spread is doing load-bearing work for the horizontal flocking rules. Left alone deliberately, written
up in `birds.rs` where the next person will hit it. Mentioned only so you do not think it was missed.
