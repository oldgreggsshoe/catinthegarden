24 September - Plan: get the ocean to the Sea of Thieves standard

SOURCE: Rare's own SIGGRAPH 2018 talk, "The Technical Art of Sea of Thieves"
https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2018-Talks-Ang_The-Technical-Art-of-Sea-of-Thieves.pdf

WHAT SEA OF THIEVES ACTUALLY DOES (primary source only)
1. Waves: Tessendorf FFT ocean (2001), not a handful of Gerstner waves.
2. Colour: stylised, not PBR. It blends a deep-water colour with a
   subsurface colour. The weights come from view angle, sun direction and a
   "wave peak mask". The mask is the FFT choppiness (sideways) offset: where
   that offset is large there is a peak, and light has less water to cross.
3. Foam: made where the FFT folds (Tessendorf's Jacobian), plus a foam ring
   wherever an object cuts the surface, found by depth comparison inside a
   window centred on the camera. That foam buffer is blurred with feedback
   each frame, so foam spreads and fades over time. The result is mixed with
   hand-painted foam textures. Calm seas only get foam around objects;
   storms get a lot of foam.
4. Sun: an area-light specular (Karis 2013 closest point on a sphere), which
   gives the big, wide low-sun reflection.
5. Underwater: a Snell's window.
6. Deck/stream water: a separate small GPU shallow-water sim. Out of scope.

WHY OURS FALLS SHORT (from the 23 Sept isolation, test-runs/ocean_sot_detail_2026-09-23)
- We sum 18 Gerstner waves plus 3 normal-only ripples. The six short waves
  (32m down to 7m) produce the regular diagonal lattice; masking them makes
  the lattice disappear. Re-aiming them only rotates it. That is the ceiling
  for any small hand-picked wave set, however carefully it is tuned.
- The foreground reads "rounded/viscous": no sideways choppiness (horizontal
  transport is OFF so buoyancy stays in sync), and too few waves.
- Foam is analytic and instant, with no memory, so it can't trail or spread.
  A texture-only foam trial on 23 Sept gave streaks and was rejected.
So the problem is structural. More tuning of Gerstner constants will not
reach this standard. The FFT is the step that matters; everything else
builds on it.

THE PLAN (one phase per session, each measured before/after on the Quadro)

Phase A - acceptance metrics before any code
  - Replays: ocean_deck_reference (low eye), ocean_manual_grid (overhead
    lattice), ocean_rough_horizon (storm), ocean_hybrid_close.
  - Lattice metric (tier 2): take a 2D FFT of the overhead capture's water
    region and measure the ratio of the strongest off-centre peak to the
    median. Today's lattice should score high; the target is near-isotropic.
  - Frame-time baseline: interleaved Immediate-present pairs, as usual.
  - Ian captures 3-4 Sea of Thieves reference shots (calm, normal, storm,
    low sun) to use as the look target.

Phase B - GPU FFT wave field (the core change)
  - Compute shaders (already proven on this card by the forest pass).
  - 3 cascades at 256x256, tile sizes chosen so they don't line up
    (for example 1000m / 237m / 53m), so the repeat can't be seen.
  - JONSWAP spectrum with directional spreading. Wind speed and fetch come
    from the existing weather/fetch coupling, so storms still work.
  - Per frame: spectrum -> time evolve -> inverse FFT (height, x/z
    displacement, derivatives) -> textures with mips.
  - Replace the 18 Gerstner + 3 ripple sums inside the camera-local patch
    with texture samples, and apply them in the tangent plane of the
    sphere. That should cost less than 21 sine sums per vertex and pixel.
  - Budget: the FFT must fit in <= 1.0ms, and total ocean time must not rise.
  - Pass: the lattice metric drops, and the overhead and low views both
    look better. A lattice fix that removes detail does not count.

Phase C - buoyancy and collision parity
  - The CPU runs the same spectrum and seed through an FFT of the largest
    cascade only, at 64x64 (rustfft). The two small cascades are under
    about 0.3m and go into the tolerance.
  - With choppiness on, finding the height at a point needs a 2-3 step
    fixed-point inversion of the horizontal offset.
  - Turn horizontal transport back on.
  - Pass: GPU/CPU surface parity test, ship float, surface camera 2.5m
    deck clearance.

Phase D - near mesh density
  - Make the camera-local grid denser near the eye, using rings or a
    projected grid with geomorphing, so choppy crests get enough vertices
    and the "broad smooth foreground faces" go.
  - Frame-time gate as always.

Phase E - Sea of Thieves shading
  - Replace today's crest-transmission layers with Rare's model: deep
    colour <-> subsurface colour, weighted by the choppiness peak mask,
    view angle and sun direction.
  - Area-light sun specular (Karis) for the wide low-sun reflection.
  - Keep the Snell's window and underside work we already have.

Phase F - temporal foam
  - A persistent foam texture centred on the camera, re-centred as the
    camera moves. It is fed by FFT folding (Jacobian < threshold) and
    object/shore intersection (depth test), and blurred plus decayed with
    feedback each frame.
  - Mix it with a tileable authored foam texture.
  - Sea-state rules: calm = foam around objects only; storm = heavy foam.
  - Pass: foam coverage by sea state (tier 2), and foam that trails and
    fades across frames.

Phase G - far field and horizon
  - Beyond the patch, use the largest cascade's low mips for normals only,
    then a filtered roughness/sheen, so the horizon doesn't shimmer or
    alias. The planet shell stays flat.

Phase H - art-direction pass against Ian's reference shots
  - Colours, foam texture, sun lobe width. Then judges, with clear/storm/
    low-sun sets.

ORDER / RISK
- B is the big one. After B, C is required before anything ships
  (buoyancy). E and F only look right on top of B.
- Biggest risks: FFT cost on the M1000M (measure in B before anything
  else) and choppy-inversion parity (C).
- Everything goes on a branch. Nothing is promoted without the replays,
  the metric and a frame-time comparison.
