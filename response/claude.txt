27 September - Brief for Codex: find and fix the "sideways white shards" on big FFT-ocean crests

Read AGENTS.md and the latest sections of docs/HANDOFF.md first (from "27 September - Spiky 'mountain' sea" onward). Branch experiment/ocean-wind-sea-spectrum, HEAD dc10e98 or later. Pull first.

Symptom
- With a big swell, e.g.
    CATINGARDEN_OCEAN_FFT=1 CATINGARDEN_OCEAN_FFT_SWELL=30 target/release/catinthegarden-app
  jagged, sideways-pointing white shards appear along the sides and faces of big foamy crests: saw-tooth wedges, as if triangles were stretched sideways.
- Evidence on the current limiter: test-runs/manual/1790525174-746683/screenshots/capture-001..003.png (the white wedges pointing left along the crest face).
- Do NOT use test-runs/manual/1790520906-735221 as evidence: it was taken with a slope-aware fold floor that has since been reverted (316d3ba).

What is already known (all measured on the CPU surface, not the GPU)
- The fold limiter is the fixed Jacobian floor MIN_JACOBIAN = 0.1: ocean_fft.rs fold_scale, mirrored by ocean_fft_fold_scale in shared_planet.wgsl, and used in the foam shader.
- A slope-dependent floor was tried and reverted. It made the per-point limit jump between a crest's tip and flanks, which sheared neighbouring vertices past each other: 120x more mesh fold-overs.
- Measured on a 0.5m CPU grid of the drawn mesh (x0 - cD):
  - fold-overs are 0.0004% of cells at swell 8 and 0.0041% at swell 30 (ignored instrument `drawn_fold_census`; regression test `the_drawn_sea_almost_never_turns_inside_out`)
  - double folds (collapsed in both directions, orientation preserved) were effectively 0 by a symmetric-part eigenvalue check
  - raising the fixed floor (0.15/0.2/0.3) makes fold-overs worse, not better
- So the CPU mesh at 0.5m barely folds. The shards are either something the CPU grid can't see, or not geometry at all.

Suspects, in the order I would test them
1. GPU mesh resolution: distant ocean chunks have vertex spacing of metres, and the vertex stage filters the cascades to about 2x the vertex spacing (ocean_surface_fft, vertex_filter). A displacement field sampled coarsely can invert triangles that the 0.5m CPU grid does not.
2. Foam drawn stretched rather than inverted geometry: the foam history atlas has 2m texels in label coordinates (ocean_foam.wgsl, ocean_history_coverage), and fold foam is per pixel. On strongly pinched crests, label-space foam is squeezed into thin wedges in drawn space.
3. The fine cascade (under 3m) displaces vertices on the GPU (chop_tangent includes fine_displacement * fine_weight) but the CPU mirror omits it.
4. Back faces being shaded as tops (planet.wgsl ocean_back_face_is_fold) hiding inverted triangles that would otherwise show as dark undersides.

How to work
- Reproduce first: a deterministic scenario, a storm or big-swell view at 20-60m eye height looking across big crests, that shows the shards in its captures.
- Then split geometry from foam with diagnostics: spray off (CATINGARDEN_OCEAN_FFT_SPRAY=0), foam off, and a render debug mode that tints back faces or inverted triangles, with a pixel count.
- Measure on the GPU image or mesh, not only the CPU grid; the CPU grid has already been shown not to see this.
- Judge any fix by before/after measurements on the drawn mesh or image, not per-point estimates. That mistake has been made once already.

Constraints
- Keep the acute crests (tips must still come to a point) and don't bring back needles or spikes.
- No frame-time regression; measure GPU cost if you add work per vertex or pixel.
- The user's uncommitted colour experiments in shared_planet.wgsl (OCEAN_SOT_WATER_ALBEDO, OCEAN_REFLECTION_SCALE) must not be committed. Commit only your own hunks, and verify the committed tree in a separate worktree with its own CARGO_TARGET_DIR.
- Update docs/HANDOFF.md (header and a new dated section) and AGENTS.md "What exists now"; commit and push.

Done when
- You can name the cause, with a measurement that shows it.
- A fix removes the shards in the reproducing scenario, backed by a before/after metric.
- the_drawn_sea_almost_never_turns_inside_out still passes, and a new regression check covers the actual cause.
