The low green areas are mostly flat because the generator deliberately puts almost all of its strong relief behind a mountain-region gate.

In `crates/baker/src/terrain.rs`:
- Lowland land gets only about 360–980 m of broad height, ~900 m of foothill noise, and ~70 m fine breakup.
- Strong ridges/peaks are multiplied by `range_gate`, which is driven by the low-frequency `mountain_region`; outside those belts it contributes only its 20% floor.
- Biome classification labels terrain above 2,400 m as mountain rock/snow, so visibly rugged terrain stops being green.
- The 256-iteration erosion/hydrology pass further softens the lowland relief.

The existing coverage instrument confirms the imbalance: only 2 of 272,856 land samples pass the 30-degree / 2 km mountain test (0.00% when rounded).

So this is not a renderer/LOD fault. It is a generator/material coupling: green is currently synonymous with low, gently varying terrain, while the strong hills are concentrated in sparse mountain belts. The smallest visual correction would be to add a broader, lower-amplitude rolling-hill field to `lowland` and leave the high mountain families unchanged; I have not edited or rebaked yet.
