Recommendation: do not increase the random terrain-noise gain yet. The main bottleneck is source coverage: outside the sparse high-resolution corridor, low flight often renders about 251/256 fallback chunks from global L4 data (~3.9 km sample spacing). More noise would decorate coarse geometry and risk recreating the blanket/spike artefacts.

Do this in order:

1. Add a bounded camera-centred raster clipmap/near-flight ring set (2–3 rings) that keeps L5–L10+ source tiles resident around the camera, with source-aware split/merge and strict upload/chunk budgets.
2. Re-capture at 10 m, 100 m, 1 km, and 10 km. Require fallback count, seam delta, surface p90/max error, detail correlation, and frame time to improve.
3. Only then add directional mid-frequency relief: coherent mountain-belt/desert/coast masks driving 4–256 m octaves, with the existing exact CPU/GPU hash and continuous source-spacing fade. Avoid global random long-wave gain.
4. Add 1–16 m material/normal detail last; it should complement geometry, not hide missing macro detail.

The first implementation should therefore be the bounded near-flight clipmap. It addresses the measured limitation and makes every later detail decision visually trustworthy; the same source-window policy can then be ported to the ray path.
