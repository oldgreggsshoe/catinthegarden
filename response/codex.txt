I’d make the emptiness part of the game: **an expedition across a genuinely vast planet**, not a uniform scatter of buildings. The strongest loop for what we already have is: take a job in a village, plan a route, travel by road/ship/air through weather and difficult terrain, discover or deliver something, and get home. The journey—not merely the destination—becomes the challenge.

I’d build it in layers:
1. **Sparse, memorable destinations:** occasional ruins, observatories, wrecks, caves, remote stations and unusual geological sites. Each should have a reason to visit and a small story or useful discovery, not just loot.
2. **Travel decisions:** fuel/range, daylight, storms, steep roads, tunnels and water crossings create meaningful route choices. Remote shelters and repair points become valuable without filling every valley.
3. **Survey and discovery:** map previously unknown terrain, photograph or scan rare formations, weather phenomena and wrecks, then bring findings back to a village. A discovery log gives exploration lasting value.
4. **A living but thinly populated world:** a few moving vehicles, ships and weather-driven incidents—stranded travellers, closed routes, missing expeditions—make the network feel used. Spawn these near relevant routes, not everywhere.
5. **Protected wilderness:** leave large stretches genuinely empty. Long sightlines, silence, storms and isolation create scale; high-interest places should be deliberately rare.

My recommended first playable slice is one village noticeboard, three expedition types (delivery, survey, rescue), one remote destination per type, and a map that records where the player has actually been. That tests whether travelling across this planet is fun before investing in hundreds of authored sites.
