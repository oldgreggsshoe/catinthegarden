I’ve read all eight pages of the review. **I broadly agree: keep the renderer and measure its expensive work rather than rewrite it.** It correctly distinguishes a source review from a benchmark and avoids promising a modern-GPU FPS multiplier.

The strongest recommendations are:
- Fixed, repeatable scenes covering land, forest, coast, roads and tunnels, including movement.
- Separating presentation/compositor stalls from rendering work.
- Checking hidden ocean computation before changing terrain architecture. A separate, uncommitted FFT-visibility experiment is already present here; it still needs its own validation.

I would tighten three things:
1. **Frame-pacing measurements need better sampling.** Current render profiles are emitted only on the0.5-second logging cadence, not every frame. Those logs cannot establish reliable1% lows or catch all streaming stalls. Record every frame’s wall-clock interval cheaply, and exclude warm-up/capture frames.
2. **Move road excavation/rendering higher up the investigation list.** Off-thread route building does not make terrain-cut queries or road shading free. Separate planning/publication, road meshes and excavation costs instead of treating “roads off” as one explanation.
3. **The road/tunnel benchmark instructions need their launch flags.** Merely changing the sample command’s scenario name will not enable roads. The network tunnel needs `PLANET_ROAD_EXPERIMENT=1` and the correct `PLANET_ROAD_NETWORK_SITE=longest_undersea`; verify the view is actually enclosed before interpreting results.

I’d treat the hardware examples as reference tiers, not buying advice or proof of1080p/60. The P50 remains useful for finding waste, even if it should not define the highest-quality target.

**My order:** improve frame-time recording, measure the five scene families, then optimise the largest demonstrated cost. The review is a good roadmap—not yet evidence that a hardware upgrade alone solves performance.
