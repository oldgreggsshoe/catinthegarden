# Round 17 — 2.0/10. The score went down.

Jeffrey 2, Marjorie 2, Osmond 2. Round 11 at this same viewpoint was 2.7
(3.0 / 2.6 / 2.5). Judged on the shipped build at `d811ff5`: the round 15
short-wave lift on, crevasses off, frame 81.37ms, all scenario assertions pass.

**Take the drop as flat-to-worse, not as a measured regression.** Judges are
fresh instances each round and their scores are not repeatable to a tenth. What
is worth reading is that three independent observers, with no shared photographs
and no knowledge of each other, named nearly the same list.

## What all three said

1. **No rock.** All three, first. "Half the frames are wall-to-wall white"; the
   photographs are "half dark rock, scree and brown moraine".
2. **The grey patches read as stains printed on the surface.** "Smudges",
   "flat stains or mould", "leopard spots with no edges, no depth, no cast
   shadow". This is the material detail tint that round 13 priced at 7.80ms and
   named as the leopard dapple. Three strangers found it unprompted.
3. **A repeating fish-scale pattern**, upper-left of pictures 1, 2, 6, 7 and 8.
   Two judges gave the same location independently.
4. **Shadows are neutral grey, never blue.** All three. Round 13 measured why:
   sky fill is 0.3-0.6% of surface light, so there is no coloured fill to show.
5. **Distant peaks are flat violet or purple slabs** with "an odd pink strip
   along their base" — the pink horizon band round 13 traced to distant brown
   lowland crossing a magenta midpoint under the distance mist.
6. **No haze with distance, no sense of scale.**

## The finding that matters most

Two judges called the near foreground **"combed parallel ripples"** and "ripples
like wind-blown sand". That is round 15's short-wave lift. It measurably widened
the tonal range, and it did make the ground rough rather than waxy -- but the
roughness it adds is too regular, and it reads as corduroy rather than as snow.
Isotropic value noise at one scale has a grain, and now that there is enough
amplitude to see it, the grain is visible.

So the fix is half right: the amplitude was correct, the character is not.

## The gap nobody can shade their way out of

The camera stands on ground that is **93.8% ice** in the seventeen texels around
it, with the nearest rock outcrop off-frame. The reference photographs are taken
from a rock ridge looking across a glacier with rock walls on both sides — they
are half rock by area. No amount of snow shading closes that, because the
subject is different. Before another round is spent, the viewpoint should be
re-sited to somewhere that holds **both** ice and rock in frame, the way every
one of the fifteen photographs does.

## Next, in order

1. **Re-site the camera** to a rock-and-ice viewpoint. Largest gap, and it is a
   scenario change rather than a renderer change.
2. **The detail tint** is costing 7.80ms to produce the stains three judges
   independently disliked. Removing it on snow is a saving and a fix at once.
3. **The fish-scale repeat** has a definite location to chase.
4. **Blue shadows** need the fill light round 13 showed is absent.
