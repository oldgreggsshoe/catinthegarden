# Round 19 — 2.0/10 again. Framing was not the problem either.

Jeffrey 2, Marjorie 2, Osmond 2. Round 17, compass sweep at the all-ice site,
was also 2.0. **Choosing eight good compositions, adding a viewpoint with real
rock, and framing level instead of 30 degrees down changed the score by nothing.**

That is the useful result. Two hypotheses died here: that the camera was in the
wrong place, and that it was pointing the wrong way. Neither was what the judges
were marking down.

## What all three said this time

1. **There is no rock.** Third round running, first item every time. The ridge
   views were added specifically to answer this and did not: "sooty grey flecked
   with white blobs", "grainy speckle, not rock". The photographs are "half dark
   cliff, ridge and grit".
2. **Ridges are rounded.** "Like whipped cream", "a snowdrift or a sand hill,
   not a mountain", "white putty". Osmond: *"The photos' ridges are always
   toothed."* All three, independently.
3. **A leopard-spot mottle** on pictures 3, 4, 5 and 8, "repeated at a single
   size across the whole face", "reads as a texture printed on, not as ground".
4. **A straight pale seam** down the left edge of picture 4. All three found it.
5. **Flat rectangular patches and facets** on the mid-slopes of 2, 6 and 7.
6. **The distant peak is a flat lavender slab with a pink-orange strip beneath**
   -- the magenta horizon, now named in three consecutive rounds.
7. **No haze with distance**, and **shadows grey rather than blue**.
8. **No glacier**: no tongue, no medial stripes, no crevasse fields.

## The uncomfortable one

Osmond, on picture 6's foreground: *"long dark streaks curving down the near
slope, more like combed hair or ripples than snow."* That is round 15's
short-wave lift, and round 17 flagged it too.

So round 15 measurably widened the tonal range and removed the waxy smear, and
what it put there instead reads as **mottle and combing**. Both states were
disliked. The amplitude was the right call and the character is wrong: a lift
that is flat across 1-12m gives the ground one dominant grain, and the eye finds
it immediately.

## Where this leaves the score

Three rounds have now been spent on shading, siting and framing without moving
2.0. The judges' first two complaints are not about materials or light:

- **rock that reads as rock**, and
- **ridges with edges on them**.

Both are *shape*. The macro terrain here comes from L4 baked data at 3,044m
texels, smoothed by erosion, and nothing downstream of it can add a cliff or a
toothed arete -- the runtime ladder adds isotropic noise, which is precisely the
mottle they are objecting to. The next real gain is in the terrain itself,
either from the baker or from a runtime rule that produces *anisotropic*
structure: ridgelines, faces, strata. That is a bigger piece of work than
anything attempted in rounds 13-19, and it is the one the evidence now points at.

Smaller, concrete, and cheap by comparison, each named by every judge:
the seam line, the flat facets, the magenta distant peaks, and the missing haze.
