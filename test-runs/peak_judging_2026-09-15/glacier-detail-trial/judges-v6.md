# V6 independent visual review — 20 September 2026

Three independent Luna agents, fresh contexts. Each inspected its own unchanged
five-photo Aletsch reference set and the eight PNGs in
`alpine_survey_8_directions/1789894404-10279/screenshots`.
No code, previous scores, other reference sets or other judges' conclusions supplied.
Rubric: full-set photographic realism of snow, glacier ice, rock, mountain forms
and lighting; ignore UI and exact geography/viewpoint. 10 = photographic realism,
9.5 = nearly indistinguishable. Visible observations only, not technical diagnoses.

## Jeffrey reference set

SCORE: 2.5/10

SUMMARY: Across all eight views, the scene is dominated by near-white, overexposed snow with soft, smeared shading and repeated stippled/paint-like patterns rather than readable snow layering, crevasses, or glacier flow. Exposed rock is mostly gray diffuse streaks without the sharp fractured faces, varied mineral color, and scale seen in the photographs. Mountain silhouettes are occasionally recognizable but generally low-detail and faceted; lighting lacks the photographic contrast and atmospheric depth of the references. The set does not convincingly reproduce alpine snow, ice, rock, or glacier scenery.

## Marjorie reference set

SCORE: 3/10

SUMMARY: The captures suggest snowy alpine terrain, but do not convincingly resemble the reference photographs. Snow is heavily overexposed and largely featureless, with repetitive stippled patterns and blurred, smeared rock patches. Glacier ice lacks the references’ crevasses, blue depth, flow structure, and layered debris. Distant mountain silhouettes are soft and simplified rather than sharply articulated alpine forms. Lighting is flat and washed out, obscuring material separation and natural contrast.

## Osmond reference set

SCORE: 2/10

SUMMARY: The game captures convey snowy mountain slopes and broad alpine forms, but they do not closely resemble the reference photographs. Snow is overwhelmingly blown out and lacks convincing ice, crevasse, and granular detail. Exposed rock appears as blurry, high-contrast dark patches rather than layered geological faces. Distant peaks are angular and low-detail, while lighting is flat and washed out. The overall impression is a stylized white terrain rather than photographic alpine glacier scenery.

**Mean: 2.5/10.** No demonstrated overall realism improvement. These are visual
assessments, not proof of any particular exposure/shader defect.
