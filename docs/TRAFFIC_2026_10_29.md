# Resident NPC road traffic — 29 October 2026

## Behaviour and scope

Timber cargo carts and covered carriages drive the actual engineered road mesh.
They have spoked rolling wheels, front steering, a seated driver and a violet
magic motor orb pushing from just behind the rear board at number-plate height
(moved there from ahead of the cart on 30 October), matching the player's established vehicle rather than adding
unanimated horses. Traffic starts automatically with the streamed road network
in normal interactive play.

- A* minimises engineered 3D road length, with admissible Euclidean distance.
  Real shared 3D endpoints join; crossing decks at different heights do not.
- Vehicles keep left (right-hand-drive convention), accelerate gently, brake
  ahead of curves and grades, and queue behind NPCs and the player. Cargo carts
  cap at 21.6 km/h, covered carriages at 28.8 km/h, lower in tight bends.
- Junctions have exclusive reservations, through priority, waiting-time promotion
  and blocked-exit checks. Village courts reserve their whole turning manoeuvre.
- Named village journeys stop for 8–20 s and make a supported 6 m radius turn
  inside the existing 40 m parking court. No U-turns at streaming frontiers.
- The player's driving controller sweeps against NPC bodies; NPCs yield to the
  player's land/driving position. No crash damage, impulse physics or pedestrian
  body collision is introduced.
- Snapshot updates remap visible agents by position/heading rather than
  respawning them in front of the camera. Unmatched nearby vehicles stop until
  they can be remapped or retire out of sight. New arrivals spawn behind the
  view or beyond 1 km after the initial fade-in.

This is **bounded resident traffic**, not a global persistent transport economy.
Only already generated roads are usable, never red schematic lines. A bounded
pool of nearby village/frontier itineraries gives vehicles meaningful local
journeys; frontiers represent onward roads, not a persistent remote destination.
Vehicles outside the visible neighbourhood recycle. Residency changes can
reroute a vehicle. Destination economy, cargo deliveries, individual persistent
identities, horses, overtaking and fully prioritised multi-lane junctions remain
future work. Conservative support checks may omit an unsafe route rather than
putting a coach off a deck or under a low overpass.

## Cost boundaries and integration

`traffic.rs` owns graph extraction, A*, supported left-lane trajectories, speed
profiles, the replaceable worker job and a 30 Hz fixed-step simulation. It uses
published `Arc<Field>` data without changing road engineering, precompute
fingerprints, terrain excavation or route planning. At most one worker, 16 cached
itineraries, 160,000 total samples, 40,000 samples per itinerary and 64 agents.
Default population is 32. A* and body-support sampling never run per frame.

NPC motion, acceleration, braking and parking/junction timers now advance on the
**scaled scene clock**, not the camera's real-time movement delta. Comma/period
slow/fast-forward traffic, and F10 freezes it without changing its interpolated
pose. Independent planet-rotation speed controls do not retime traffic. Existing
fixed-timestep scenarios still use their own scene time, unchanged at 1x.

The old 0.25-second input clamp silently saturated accelerated traffic (already
at 10x on a 15 FPS frame). The fixed 1/30 game-second substeps now accept up to
**8 game seconds per rendered frame** (~240 ticks). Tests compare 0.1x through
40x at 15 FPS with equivalent elapsed-time reference journeys, including arrival
and dwell timing. No larger unsafe physics step or junction skip is introduced.
At extreme multipliers/hitches above that budget, excess time is discarded;
traffic cannot keep exact pace with the largest clock rungs. This is deliberate
FPS protection, with no retained catch-up debt after slowing/pausing. Diagnostic
population logs stay on a two-second **wall-clock** cadence, not accelerated I/O.


The render thread advances bounded local following/reservation rules with smooth
interpolation. `traffic_render.rs` uploads at most 64 96-byte instances and uses
two cached meshes/two draws, with distance/view culling. `traffic.wgsl` reuses the
existing filtered timber tile and exact road lantern flicker helpers. Inside
bores only the real wall lamps light the vehicles; outside they receive sunlight,
eclipse/moon illumination and ambient light. No extra shadow or light pass.
These are modest procedural vehicle models, not a photorealistic art pass.

`PLANET_TRAFFIC=0` or `PLANET_DISABLE=traffic` disables simulation.
`PLANET_TRAFFIC_COUNT=N` selects 0–64 vehicles. Existing replays default off so
they retain their historical workloads; use `PLANET_TRAFFIC=1` explicitly for
an A/B benchmark. `road_traffic` defaults on and follows an NPC;
`road_traffic_tunnel` follows one inside a bore (use the `longest_undersea` site).
Their population
and travelled-distance assertions fail for an empty/static scene.
`PLANET_TRAFFIC_WAIT=1` waits for the initial worker, for repeatable captures;
normal startup stays asynchronous.
